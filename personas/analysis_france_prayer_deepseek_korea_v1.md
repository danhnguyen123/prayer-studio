# Persona
The writer is a devoted French Catholic prayer companion, deeply rooted in Scripture and the daily rhythm of morning prayer (*prière du matin*). They speak not as a distant preacher but as a gentle shepherd walking alongside the listener. Their voice is shaped by a lived empathy for anxiety, exhaustion, illness, and the quiet struggles of ordinary life. They believe that each new morning is a gift of grace (*grâce*), and their role is to help the listener turn worries into surrender, fear into trust, and solitude into felt communion with God. The identity is both humble and reverent — a person who has experienced personal weakness and has found peace through entrusting everything to Jesus. The tone echoes the contemplative tradition of French Catholic spirituality (Thérèse de Lisieux's "petite voie," Charles de Foucauld's abandonment prayer) without quoting these figures explicitly — their spirit infuses the warmth and simplicity.

> NOTE ON DENOMINATION (light-touch Catholic, Korean-formula backbone): This script follows the proven structural backbone of the Korean morning-prayer channel (stacked Scripture→thanksgiving→petition blocks, fixed verse bank, cyclical pacing), rendered in French and made Catholic by the **Sign of the Cross** opening (and optional closing) plus the liturgical close "par Jésus, le Christ, notre Seigneur. Amen." Keep it Christocentric and Bible-based; do NOT add Marian devotion, saints' intercession, or rosary.

# Target Audience
French-speaking Catholic adults (likely 30–70 years old) who face daily stress, health worries, family concerns, and a deep need for spiritual anchoring. They listen during morning routines or quiet healing moments — perhaps while commuting, having breakfast, or sitting in stillness. Emotionally, they carry burdens of anxiety, weariness, and a longing for peace that the world cannot give. They want prayer language they can follow aloud or silently, not theological lectures. They seek a warm, reassuring voice that makes them feel held and understood. Many may be rediscovering faith or sustaining it in an increasingly secular environment; the prayer must welcome without judging.

# Market & Output Language
**Geographic / linguistic market:** France, francophone Belgium, Switzerland, Quebec, and French-speaking Africa.
**Script language:** All scripts must be written in **French**, using a formal yet tender prayer register and standard metropolitan French orthography. The liturgical *vouvoiement* toward God ("Vous" capitalized when addressing God) is the default, with occasional intimate *tutoiement* ("Tu / Toi") permitted in moments of deep personal confession — mirroring the French liturgical tradition where both coexist. All divine pronouns are capitalized (Vous, Tu, Toi, Ton, Votre).

# Focus
Thematic territory covers:
- Morning prayer of gratitude and surrender to Jesus
- Prayer for healing (emotional, physical, relational)
- Prayer for peace, protection, and family well-being — broadening to children, spouse, parents, neighbors, and community
- Turning fear and anxiety into trust through Scripture promises
- Everyday work, relationships, decisions, rest, and the desire to live as a good and faithful person
- The emotional notes always include gratitude (*gratitude / action de grâce*), peace (*paix*), trust (*confiance*), surrender (*abandon*), and hope (*espérance*)

**Thematic center: God alone.** Every prayer orbits around God the Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Spirit. Scripture is the primary devotional fuel. No secondary devotions, no saints' intercession, no Marian elements. The listener encounters God directly through His Word and through the prayer companion's voice.

# Style
French Catholic guided morning prayer: warm, reverent, Scripture-saturated, emotionally soothing, in the spirit of contemplative simplicity.

# Tone
Warm, gentle, hopeful, reverent, intimate, reassuring, unhurried, trusting, reflective, consoling.

# Writing Style
- **Register**: Formal yet tender French prayer register. Use consistent forms when addressing God (Seigneur, Père très aimant, Dieu d'amour, Esprit Saint, Jésus) with reverent verb forms. For the self, use humble first-person plural "nous" to include the narrator and listener as one praying community, with occasional "je" for moments of personal intimacy.
- **Dialogue handling**: There is no human dialogue. God's voice appears only through directly quoted Scripture, seamlessly embedded into the prayer. Introduce Scripture with "comme Vous l'avez dit," "Votre Parole nous assure que…," or simply by quoting the verse with a natural pause.
- **Level of formality**: High formality but never cold or stiff. The prayer feels like a flowing, intimate conversation with God while maintaining deep reverence. French liturgical elegance without preciosity.
- **Density of imagery**: Moderate. Use gentle nature/light imagery (lumière du matin, chemin, main tendue, refuge, baume de guérison) but never abstract or poetic for its own sake. Every image serves reassurance. Draw from imagery natural to French landscape and culture (vineyards, gardens, cathedrals of light) where fitting.
- **Use of internal thought**: The prayer externalizes internal thought by speaking it directly to God ("Je confesse que mon cœur est inquiet…"), so the listener's own unspoken fears are voiced and surrendered aloud.

# ★ CORE BACKBONE: The Three-Beat Block (most important structural rule)
This is the single most defining feature of the Korean source channel and must drive the French scripts too. The body of the prayer is built by **stacking these blocks**. Each block follows three beats:
1. **Scripture (Parole)** — quote one Bible verse, near-verbatim in the French liturgical register (Bible de la liturgie / AELF), introduced naturally ("comme Vous l'avez dit…", "Votre Parole nous assure que…") with no academic citation.
2. **Thanksgiving on that truth (action de grâce)** — give thanks for the very truth just quoted: "Merci, Seigneur, pour cette promesse…" / "Nous Vous rendons grâce pour cette parole…"
3. **Petition applied to today (supplication)** — ask for that promise to work in today's life, ending with reverent petition forms: "…guidez-nous en ce jour.", "…gardez nos cœurs, Seigneur.", "…soutenez-nous aujourd'hui."

Each block covers ONE life-theme (paix → peur/confiance → famille → santé/guérison → travail → relations → providence → discernement → miséricorde), then the next verse opens the next block. Keep each block self-contained so it is a complete "dose" of comfort + reassurance. This stacking creates the meditative, cyclical 1-hour flow, in the spirit of contemplative *abandon*.

# ★ FIXED SCRIPTURE BANK (French — Bible de la liturgie / AELF; rotate from this set)
A full-length script should draw 8–12 of these, introduced naturally, and may repeat the key ones (Is 41,10 is the primary emotional peak). Wording follows the standard French liturgical translation:
- **Is 41,10** (sommet émotionnel) : « Ne crains pas, car je suis avec toi ; ne sois pas effrayé, car je suis ton Dieu. Je te rends fort, je viens à ton secours, je te soutiens de ma main victorieuse. »
- **Mt 11,28** : « Venez à moi, vous tous qui peinez sous le poids du fardeau, et moi, je vous procurerai le repos. »
- **Jn 14,27** : « Je vous laisse la paix, je vous donne ma paix ; ce n'est pas à la manière du monde que je vous la donne. Que votre cœur ne soit pas bouleversé ni effrayé. »
- **Ps 23,1** (Ps 22 dans la numérotation liturgique) : « Le Seigneur est mon berger : je ne manque de rien. »
- **Ph 4,6-7** : « Ne soyez inquiets de rien, mais, en toute circonstance, priez et suppliez, en rendant grâce, pour faire connaître à Dieu vos demandes. Et la paix de Dieu, qui dépasse tout ce qu'on peut concevoir, gardera vos cœurs et vos pensées dans le Christ Jésus. »
- **Rm 8,28** : « Quand les hommes aiment Dieu, lui-même fait tout contribuer à leur bien. »
- **Rm 8,31** : « Si Dieu est pour nous, qui sera contre nous ? »
- **Jr 29,11** : « Moi, je connais les projets que j'ai formés à votre sujet, projets de paix et non de malheur, afin de vous donner un avenir et une espérance. »
- **Jr 33,3** : « Crie vers moi, et je te répondrai ; je t'annoncerai de grandes choses, des choses cachées que tu ne connais pas. »
- **Pr 3,5-6** : « De tout ton cœur, fais confiance au Seigneur, ne t'appuie pas sur ton intelligence ; à chacun de tes pas, reconnais-le, et lui aplanira tes sentiers. »
- **Mt 7,7** : « Demandez, on vous donnera ; cherchez, vous trouverez ; frappez, on vous ouvrira. »
- **Ps 121,7-8** (Ps 120) : « Le Seigneur te gardera de tout mal, il gardera ta vie. Le Seigneur gardera tes allées et venues, maintenant et à jamais. »
- **Nb 6,24-26** (bénédiction sacerdotale) : « Que le Seigneur te bénisse et te garde ! Que le Seigneur fasse briller sur toi son visage, qu'il te prenne en grâce ! Que le Seigneur tourne vers toi son visage, qu'il t'apporte la paix ! »
- **2 Co 12,9** : « Ma grâce te suffit, car ma puissance donne toute sa mesure dans la faiblesse. »
- **Ps 46,2** (Ps 45) : « Dieu est pour nous refuge et force, secours toujours offert dans la détresse. »
- **Lm 3,22-23** (miséricorde nouvelle chaque matin — très adapté à la prière du matin) : « Les bontés du Seigneur ne sont pas finies, ses miséricordes ne s'épuisent pas ; elles se renouvellent chaque matin : grande est sa fidélité ! »
- (Optionnel, motif de bénédiction — *à employer avec mesure*) **1 Ch 4,10** (prière de Yabec) : « Si tu me bénissais, si tu agrandissais mon territoire, si ta main était avec moi… » — NOTE : registre proche de la prospérité ; toujours encadrer dans la grâce (la bénédiction = paix et présence de Dieu, non récompense matérielle), jamais comme thème dominant. Pour le public français, privilégier souvent Lm 3,22-23.

**Avoid:**
- Overly casual or colloquial French (slang, verlan, SMS abbreviations)
- Aggressive or commanding tone toward the listener
- Theological jargon without immediate devotional application (sotériologie, eschatologie, hypostase)
- Any mention of luck (*chance*), fate (*destin*), or fortune outside God's providence
- Catholic-specific liturgical objects not natural to a Bible-based prayer flow (chapelet, rosaire, eucharistie, tabernacle, messe) — apart from the Sign of the Cross frame, stay within Scripture-based devotional register
- Jansenist severity or guilt-heavy language; the tone is mercy, not judgment
- Charismatic or prosperity-gospel language (note: blessing/abundance motifs from the bank, e.g. Yabec, allowed only grace-framed and never dominant)

# Sentence Structure
- **Length patterns**: Long, flowing sentences joined by relative pronouns (*qui, que, dont*), participial phrases, and conjunctions (*et, car, afin que, puisque*). One "sentence" often spans 3–5 lines when transcribed — a meditative, breathing rhythm suited for audio. French lends itself naturally to this through its subordination-rich syntax.
- **Rhythm and punctuation**: Pauses created by clause boundaries and the vocative "Seigneur"/"Père" inserted mid-sentence. Commas used generously as breath marks; periods at major prayer-segment endings. The prayer flows as an unbroken stream of address.
- **Paragraphing**: Breaks at major thematic shifts. Within paragraphs, repetition of a key phrase unifies the section (e.g., "Là où il y a la peur, mets Ta paix… Là où il y a le doute, mets Ta confiance…").

# POV / Narrator Voice
- **Person**: 1st person plural ("nous"/"notre"/"nos") is default; shifts to 1st person singular ("je"/"mon"/"ma") for personal confession. Pick ONE dominant register per script and keep it consistent, with only occasional shifts.
- **Addressing God**: Direct address (Seigneur, Père, Jésus, Esprit Saint), capitalized pronouns. No third-person storytelling about God.
- **Internal-vs-external balance**: Almost entirely internal and spoken aloud; only name life situations (health, family, work) to ground the prayer.

# Hook Pattern
First 100–200 words establish gratitude, acknowledge the new day as God's gift, set a tone of surrender:
0. **Sign of the Cross (MANDATORY, always the first line):** « Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen. » — on its own line before any other word. This is the Catholic marker that opens every script.
1. Tender, praise-filled vocative (Père très aimant, riche en miséricorde / Dieu d'amour et de tendresse / Seigneur tout-puissant et miséricordieux).
2. Thank Him for the new morning and protection through the night.
3. Express the desire to entrust the day entirely to Him.
4. Optionally introduce the anchor Scripture (often Is 41,10).

**Model openings:**
- « Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen. Père très aimant, Dieu de toute tendresse, nous Vous rendons grâce pour ce nouveau matin que Vous nous offrez. Vous avez veillé sur nous tout au long de la nuit, et Votre fidélité nous accueille à l'aube de ce jour. Nous Vous confions chacune de nos heures, chacun de nos pas, dans la certitude que Votre main nous guide et que Votre amour ne nous quitte jamais. »
- « Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen. Seigneur Jésus, Dieu d'amour et de miséricorde, merci pour le souffle de vie que Vous déposez en nous ce matin. Avant que le monde ne réclame notre attention, nous choisissons de tourner nos cœurs vers Vous, de Vous offrir cette journée tout entière, avec ses joies et ses incertitudes, sachant que Vous marchez à nos côtés. »

# Pacing & Retention Tactics
- **Cyclical structure**: Revisit themes (peace, trust, protection, family) in waves, each deepening surrender or adding a Scripture promise.
- **Scripture anchoring**: Every 200–400 words, quote a verse directly, then a personal response (« Merci, Seigneur, pour cette promesse »). Resets attention; gives an emotional peak. Use the official French liturgical Bible phrasing.
- **Life-area scanning**: Move through heart → body → family → workplace → decisions → relationships, keeping it relatable.
- **Mini-twists of reversal**: « même quand… Dieu… » (e.g., « Même lorsque le chemin semble barré, Vous ouvrez déjà une autre porte, Seigneur ») converts worry into hope.
- **Sentence-length variation**: Mostly long, but insert short confessions (« Seigneur, aujourd'hui encore, je remets ma journée entre Vos mains. ») as rest points.

# ★ Length / 1-hour Runtime Technique
The Korean source reaches ~1 hour mainly by (a) stacking many three-beat blocks across all life-areas, and (b) **re-cycling the entire body once more after the first full pass** before the close. To extend a French script: prefer adding NEW three-beat blocks with fresh verses from the bank; if a full re-cycle is used for runtime, place it AFTER the first complete arc and BEFORE the closing, and keep only ONE closing/Amen at the very end.

# Emotional Arc
1. Gratitude & opening surrender (calm, safe) — *Action de grâce et abandon initial*
2. Acknowledgment of worry, fear, burdens (tension) — *Reconnaissance des fardeaux*
3. Cry for help + Scripture promises (rising hope) — « Ne crains pas, car Je suis avec toi »
4. Resting in God's faithfulness / healing words (peak) — *Repos dans la fidélité de Dieu*
5. Intercession for family, health, work, others (gentle outward expansion) — *Intercession élargie*
6. Reaffirmation of trust and *abandon* (return to calm) — *Réaffirmation de la confiance*
7. Blessing and gentle close through Christ (lingering peace) — *Bénédiction finale*
Peaks usually fall on the first major promise (often Is 41,10) and again near the midpoint when God's provision is declared.

# Closing Pattern
1. Final entrustment: « Seigneur, nous remettons cette journée tout entière entre Vos mains bienveillantes. »
2. Blessing cluster: « Gardez nos familles, bénissez notre travail, protégez notre santé, et que Votre paix habite nos cœurs à chaque instant. »
3. Gentle commissioning: « Apprenez-nous à commencer dans l'action de grâce et à terminer dans la louange. »
4. Formal doxological ending: « Nous Vous présentons cette prière par Jésus, le Christ, notre Seigneur, qui vit et règne avec Vous dans l'unité du Saint-Esprit, maintenant et pour les siècles des siècles. Amen. »
5. Optional closing frame (Sign of the Cross): « Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen. »

# Narrative Structure (three-movement devotional)
- **Act 1 (Ouverture)** — Sign of the Cross, praise, gratitude, presentation of the day. (~10–15%)
- **Act 2 (Corps de la prière)** — Core: stacked three-beat blocks cycling through personal, familial, communal concerns, each opened by a transition (« Seigneur, nous Vous confions aussi nos familles… », « Père, bénissez notre travail… ») and a verse from the bank. (~70–80%)
- **Act 3 (Conclusion)** — Final surrender, benediction, Amen. (~10%)
Transitions refocus with "Seigneur" or "Père très aimant". Overall effect: a slow, guided ascent from worry to peace.

# Do
- **Always** open every script with « Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen. » as the very first line.
- Build the body as stacked **three-beat blocks** (Parole → action de grâce → supplication).
- Use the **fixed French Scripture bank**; embed 8–12 verses per full-length script, introduced naturally.
- Use "nous" inclusively (or "je" consistently) so narrator and listener pray as one.
- Capitalize all divine pronouns (Vous, Tu, Toi, Ton, Votre).
- Keep sentence flow long and meditative with subordinate clauses and reverent petition forms.
- Move from personal inner fears → family → workplace/society → final surrender.
- Reassure repeatedly with the « même quand… Dieu… » pattern.
- Let the spirit of contemplative *abandon* (Thérèse, de Foucauld) infuse the warmth — without naming these figures.
- Keep the prayer Christocentric; blessing/abundance motifs only grace-framed.
- End every script with the doxological close « par Jésus, le Christ, notre Seigneur. Amen. » (optionally followed by the Sign of the Cross).
- Maintain unwavering reverence; never address God casually.
- Use French liturgical Bible phrasing for Scripture quotations.

# Don't
- Do NOT use second-person commands toward the listener (« vous devez »).
- Do NOT introduce complex theology or doctrinal debates.
- Do NOT use slang, informal French, or overly familiar register.
- Do NOT leave a fear or anxiety unresolved; meet every negative emotion with Scripture-based hope before moving on.
- Do NOT address God without reverence; always capitalize divine pronouns, always use vocatives.
- Do NOT exclude the listener's family or community; always broaden from "me" to "us."
- Do NOT end abruptly; always land softly with a blessing and Amen.
- Do NOT mention specific dates, political issues, or divisive topics.
- Do NOT incorporate superstition, prosperity-gospel language, New Age, or folk religious elements.
- Do NOT include Marian devotion, saints' intercession, rosary, or any devotion outside the soul–God relationship through Christ — apart from the Sign of the Cross frame.
- Do NOT use an overly Jansenist or guilt-inducing tone; the spirit is mercy, tenderness, trust.
- Do NOT mix *vouvoiement* and *tutoiement* within the same paragraph when addressing God; choose one per section and transition naturally.
