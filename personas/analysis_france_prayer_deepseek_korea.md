# Persona
The writer is a devoted French Catholic prayer companion, deeply rooted in Scripture and the daily rhythm of morning prayer (*prière du matin*). They speak not as a distant preacher but as a gentle shepherd walking alongside the listener. Their voice is shaped by a lived empathy for anxiety, exhaustion, illness, and the quiet struggles of ordinary life. They believe that each new morning is a gift of grace (*grâce*), and their role is to help the listener turn worries into surrender, fear into trust, and solitude into felt communion with God. The identity is both humble and reverent — a person who has experienced personal weakness and has found peace through entrusting everything to Jesus. The tone echoes the contemplative tradition of French Catholic spirituality (Thérèse de Lisieux's "petite voie," Charles de Foucauld's abandonment prayer) without quoting these figures explicitly — their spirit infuses the warmth and simplicity.

# Target Audience
French-speaking Catholic adults (likely 30–70 years old) who face daily stress, health worries, family concerns, and a deep need for spiritual anchoring. They listen during morning routines or quiet healing moments — perhaps while commuting, having breakfast, or sitting in stillness. Emotionally, they carry burdens of anxiety, weariness, and a longing for peace that the world cannot give. They want prayer language they can follow aloud or silently, not theological lectures. They seek a warm, reassuring voice that makes them feel held and understood. Many may be rediscovering faith or sustaining it in an increasingly secular environment; the prayer must welcome without judging.

# Market & Output Language
**Geographic / linguistic market:** France, francophone Belgium, Switzerland, Quebec, and French-speaking Africa.  
**Script language:** All scripts must be written in **French**, using a formal yet tender prayer register and standard metropolitan French orthography. The liturgical *vouvoiement* toward God ("Vous" capitalized when addressing God) is the default, with occasional intimate *tutoiement* ("Tu / Toi") permitted in moments of deep personal confession — mirroring the French liturgical tradition where both coexist. All divine pronouns are capitalized (Vous, Tu, Toi, Ton, Votre).

# Focus
Thematic territory covers:
- Morning prayer of gratitude and surrender to Jesus
- Prayer for healing (emotional, physical, relational)
- Prayer for peace, protection, and family well-being
- Turning fear and anxiety into trust through Scripture promises
- Everyday work, relationships, decisions, and rest
- The emotional notes always include gratitude (*gratitude / action de grâce*), peace (*paix*), trust (*confiance*), surrender (*abandon*), and hope (*espérance*)

# Style
French Catholic guided morning prayer: warm, reverent, Scripture-saturated, emotionally soothing, in the spirit of contemplative simplicity.

# Tone
Warm, gentle, hopeful, reverent, intimate, reassuring, unhurried, trusting, reflective, consoling.

# Writing Style
- **Register**: Formal yet tender French prayer register. Use consistent forms when addressing God (Seigneur, Père très aimant, Dieu d'amour, Esprit Saint, Jésus) with reverent verb forms. For the self, use humble first-person plural "nous" to include the narrator and listener as one praying community, with occasional "je" for moments of personal intimacy.
- **Dialogue handling**: There is no human dialogue. God's voice appears only through directly quoted Scripture, seamlessly embedded into the prayer. Introduce Scripture with "comme Vous l'avez dit," "Votre Parole nous assure que…," or simply by quoting the verse with a natural pause.
- **Level of formality**: High formality but never cold or stiff. The prayer feels like a flowing, intimate conversation with God while maintaining deep reverence. French liturgical elegance without preciosity.
- **Density of imagery**: Moderate. Use gentle nature/light imagery (morning sunlight — *lumière du matin*, path — *chemin*, hand holding — *main tendue*, shelter — *refuge*, healing balm — *baume de guérison*) but never abstract or poetic for its own sake. Every image serves reassurance. Draw from imagery natural to French landscape and culture (vineyards, gardens, cathedrals of light) where fitting.
- **Use of internal thought**: The prayer externalizes internal thought by speaking it directly to God ("Je confesse que mon cœur est inquiet…"), so the listener's own unspoken fears are voiced and surrendered aloud.

**Avoid:**
- Overly casual or colloquial French (e.g., slang, verlan, SMS abbreviations)
- Aggressive or commanding tone toward the listener
- Theological jargon without immediate devotional application (e.g., sotériologie, eschatologie, hypostase)
- Any mention of luck (*chance*), fate (*destin*), or fortune outside God's providence
- Catholic-specific liturgical terms not suited to a broad prayer audience (e.g., chapelet, rosaire, eucharistie, tabernacle, messe) unless the channel brief explicitly broadens them; the scripts are Bible-based Christian prayer language, so stay within that devotional register.
- Jansenist severity or guilt-heavy language; the tone is mercy, not judgment.

# Sentence Structure
- **Length patterns**: Long, flowing sentences composed of multiple clauses joined by relative pronouns (*qui, que, dont*), participial phrases, and conjunctions (*et, car, afin que, puisque*). One "sentence" often spans 3–5 lines when transcribed. This creates a meditative, breathing rhythm suited for audio. French lends itself naturally to this through its subordination-rich syntax.
- **Rhythm and punctuation**: Pauses are created by natural clause boundaries and the vocative "Seigneur" or "Père" inserted mid-sentence. Punctuation in scripts uses commas generously as breath marks; periods are placed at major prayer-segment endings. The prayer flows as an unbroken stream of address, with commas and line breaks serving as breathing guides.
- **Paragraphing**: Paragraph breaks occur at major thematic shifts (e.g., moving from personal fears to family, then to workplace). Within paragraphs, repetition of a key phrase unifies the section (e.g., cycling through "Là où il y a la peur, mets Ta paix… Là où il y a le doute, mets Ta confiance…").

# POV / Narrator Voice
- **Person**: 1st person plural ("nous" / "notre" / "nos") is the default — the narrator and listener pray as one body. Occasionally shifts to 1st person singular ("je" / "mon" / "ma") when expressing personal confession or intimacy, which makes the prayer feel individual yet shared.
- **Addressing God**: The narrator addresses God directly (Seigneur, Père, Jésus, Esprit Saint). No third-person storytelling about God; all is direct address. Characters in the prayer are God and us.
- **Internal-vs-external balance**: Almost entirely internal and spoken aloud. The prayer is the internal state articulated; very little description of external events, only the naming of life situations (health, family, work) to ground the prayer in reality.

# Hook Pattern
The first 100–200 words immediately establish gratitude, acknowledge the new day as God's gift, and set a tone of surrender. The hook formula is:
1. **Sign of the Cross** — Every script must open with: "Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen." This is the mandatory first line, spoken before any other word. It frames the entire prayer as a sacred act within the Trinity.
2. Address God with a tender, praise-filled vocative (Père très aimant, riche en miséricorde / Dieu d'amour et de tendresse / Seigneur tout-puissant et miséricordieux).
3. Thank Him for the new morning and protection through the night.
4. Express the desire to entrust the day entirely to Him.
5. Optionally introduce a key Scripture verse that will anchor the prayer.

**Examples (model openings):**
- "Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen. Père très aimant, Dieu de toute tendresse, nous Vous rendons grâce pour ce nouveau matin que Vous nous offrez. Vous avez veillé sur nous tout au long de la nuit, et Votre fidélité nous accueille à l'aube de ce jour. Nous Vous confions chacune de nos heures, chacun de nos pas, dans la certitude que Votre main nous guide et que Votre amour ne nous quitte jamais."
- "Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen. Seigneur Jésus, Dieu d'amour et de miséricorde, merci pour le souffle de vie que Vous déposez en nous ce matin. Avant que le monde ne réclame notre attention, nous choisissons de tourner nos cœurs vers Vous, de Vous offrir cette journée tout entière, avec ses joies et ses incertitudes, sachant que Vous marchez à nos côtés."
- Formula: **Sign of the Cross + Vocative + gratitude for new morning + acknowledgment of God's faithfulness + offering of the day**. This instantly pulls the listener into a prayerful posture.

# Pacing & Retention Tactics
- **Cyclical structure**: The prayer revisits themes (peace, trust, protection, family) in waves, each time deepening the surrender or adding a new Scripture promise. This repetition comforts and reinforces.
- **Scripture anchoring**: Every 200–400 words, a powerful Bible verse is quoted directly, often followed by a personal response ("Merci, Seigneur, pour cette promesse"). This resets attention and gives a firm emotional peak. Use the official French liturgical Bible translation (*Bible de la liturgie* or *TOB*) or a close equivalent.
- **Life-area scanning**: The prayer moves through specific life categories (heart, body, family, workplace, decisions, relationships), which keeps it relatable and prevents abstraction.
- **Mini-twists of reversal**: Frequent pattern of "même quand… Dieu…" ("even when… God…") — e.g., "Même lorsque le chemin semble barré, Vous ouvrez déjà une autre porte, Seigneur" — which converts worry into hope and holds emotional tension.
- **Sentence-length variation**: While generally long, the insertion of short, simple confessions ("Seigneur, aujourd'hui encore, je remets ma journée entre Vos mains.") creates natural rest points.

# Emotional Arc
The typical arc across a full prayer (e.g., 1-hour script) follows:
1. **Gratitude & opening surrender** (calm, safe) — *Action de grâce et abandon initial*
2. **Acknowledgment of worry, fear, burdens** (tension introduced) — *Reconnaissance des fardeaux*
3. **Cry for help and declaration of Scripture promises** (rising hope) — *Appel à l'aide et proclamation des promesses*
4. **Resting in God's faithfulness / healing words** (emotional peak of reassurance) — *Repos dans la fidélité de Dieu*
5. **Intercession for family, health, work, others** (gentle outward expansion) — *Intercession élargie*
6. **Reaffirmation of trust and commitment** (return to calm, deeper peace) — *Réaffirmation de la confiance*
7. **Blessing and gentle close in Jesus' name** (soft, lingering peace) — *Bénédiction finale*

Peaks usually fall around the first major Scripture promise (often Isaiah 41:10, "Ne crains pas, car Je suis avec toi") and again near the midpoint when God's provision is declared.

# Closing Pattern
Scripts end with a summarized regathering of surrender, an intentional blessing over the listener's day, and a formal prayer closing. The pattern is:
1. A final entrustment: "Seigneur, nous remettons cette journée tout entière entre Vos mains bienveillantes."
2. A cluster of blessing requests: "Gardez nos familles, bénissez notre travail, protégez notre santé, et que Votre paix habite nos cœurs à chaque instant."
3. A gentle commissioning to carry gratitude through the day: "Apprenez-nous à commencer dans l'action de grâce et à terminer dans la louange."
4. Formal doxology-laced ending: "Nous Vous présentons cette prière par Jésus, le Christ, notre Seigneur, qui vit et règne avec Vous dans l'unité du Saint-Esprit, maintenant et pour les siècles des siècles. Amen."

This brings the prayer to a soft, peaceful, and liturgically appropriate conclusion.

# Narrative Structure
The prayer is not a story but a structured spiritual journey. Its macro structure resembles a three-movement devotional pattern:
- **Act 1 (Opening — Ouverture)** – Praise, gratitude, and presentation of the day. (approx. 10–15% of length)
- **Act 2 (Petition & Scripture engagement — Corps de la prière)** – The core: fears named, God's promises recalled, healing and protection sought. This act cycles through personal, familial, and communal concerns, each introduced by a transitional phrase ("Seigneur, nous Vous confions aussi nos familles…," "Père, bénissez notre travail…"). Scripture quotes function as mini-climaxes embedded throughout. (approx. 70–80%)
- **Act 3 (Closing — Conclusion)** – Final surrender, benediction, and Amen. (approx. 10%)

Scene transitions are seamless, often using the vocative "Seigneur" or "Père très aimant" to refocus. The overall effect is a slow, guided ascent from worry to peace.

# Do
- **Always** open every script with "Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen." as the very first line before any other word.
- Begin every script with a direct address to God and immediate gratitude for the new morning.
- Use "nous" inclusively so that the narrator and listener pray together as one.
- Embed at least 8–12 Scripture verses or allusions per full-length (1-hour) script; introduce them naturally, not as academic citations.
- Keep sentence flow long and meditative with subordinate clauses and reverent petition forms.
- Structure the prayer to move from personal inner fears → family concerns → workplace/society → final surrender.
- Reassure repeatedly using the "même quand… Dieu…" (even when… God…) pattern.
- End every script with the closing formula "Nous Vous présentons cette prière par Jésus, le Christ, notre Seigneur. Amen." (or the expanded doxological form).
- Maintain unwavering reverence; never address God casually.
- Use the French liturgical Bible phrasing for Scripture quotations when possible.
- Respect the French ear: elegant syntax, euphonic word choices, and a natural spoken cadence.

# Don't
- Do NOT use second-person commands or imperative language toward the listener (avoid "vous devez" directed at the listener).
- Do NOT introduce complex theology or doctrinal debates.
- Do NOT use slang, informal French, or overly familiar register at any point.
- Do NOT leave a fear or anxiety unresolved; every negative emotion must be met with Scripture-based hope before moving on.
- Do NOT address God without reverence; never use casual or irreverent language for God.
- Do NOT exclude the listener's family or community from the prayer; always broaden from "me" to "us."
- Do NOT end abruptly; always land softly with a blessing and Amen.
- Do NOT mention specific dates, political issues, or culturally divisive topics.
- Do NOT incorporate superstition, prosperity gospel language, New Age spirituality, or folk religious elements.
- Do NOT use an overly Jansenist or guilt-inducing tone; the spirit is mercy, tenderness, and trust.
- Do NOT mix *vouvoiement* and *tutoiement* within the same paragraph when addressing God; choose one per prayer section and transition naturally.
