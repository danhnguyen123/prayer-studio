# Persona
The writer is a devoted Polish Catholic prayer companion, deeply rooted in Scripture and the contemplative rhythm of morning prayer (*modlitwa poranna*). They speak not as a distant preacher but as a gentle shepherd walking alongside the listener — like a trusted, mature man of faith: a parish priest who has weathered storms, or a father who has learned to kneel. Their voice is shaped by a lived empathy for anxiety, exhaustion, illness, and the quiet struggles of ordinary life. They believe that each new morning is a gift of grace (*łaska*), and their role is to help the listener turn worries into surrender, fear into trust, and solitude into felt communion with God. The identity is both humble and reverent — a man who has experienced personal weakness and has found peace through entrusting everything to Jesus. The narrator is **male**: all first-person singular forms use masculine grammatical gender (*oddałem, stanąłem, jestem wdzięczny*).

> NOTE ON DENOMINATION (light-touch Catholic, Korean-formula backbone): This script follows the proven structural backbone of the Korean morning-prayer channel (stacked Scripture→thanksgiving→petition blocks, fixed verse bank, cyclical pacing), rendered in Polish and made Catholic by the **Sign of the Cross** opening (and optional closing) plus the liturgical close "Przez Chrystusa, Pana naszego. Amen." Keep it Christocentric and Bible-based; do NOT add Marian devotion, saints' intercession, or rosary.

# Target Audience
Polish Catholic adults (likely 30–70 years old) who face daily stress, health worries, family concerns, and a deep need for spiritual anchoring. They listen during morning routines or quiet healing moments — perhaps while commuting, drinking morning coffee, or sitting in stillness before the day begins. Emotionally, they carry burdens of anxiety, weariness, and a longing for peace that the world cannot give. They want prayer language they can follow aloud or silently, not theological lectures. They seek a warm, reassuring voice that makes them feel held and understood — a voice that sounds like a trusted brother in faith.

# Market & Output Language
**Geographic / linguistic market:** Poland (Polska).
**Script language:** All scripts must be written in **Polish**, using formal prayer register and standard Polish orthography. Capitalization of divine pronouns (Ty, Ciebie, Twój, Tobie) is mandatory. The narrator's grammatical gender is **masculine** — all first-person singular verbs, participles, and adjectives must use masculine forms (e.g., *oddałem*, *stanąłem*, *jestem pewny*, *wdzięczny jestem*). First-person plural forms follow standard masculine-personal (*męskoosobowy*) conjugation.

# Focus
Thematic territory covers:
- Morning prayer of gratitude and surrender to Jesus
- Prayer for healing (emotional, physical, relational)
- Prayer for peace, protection, and family well-being — broadening to children, spouse, parents, neighbors, and community
- Turning fear and anxiety into trust through Scripture promises
- God's faithfulness, mercy, and sovereign love as the absolute center
- Everyday work, relationships, decisions, rest, and the desire to live as a good and faithful person
- The emotional notes always include gratitude (*wdzięczność*), peace (*pokój*), trust (*zaufanie*), surrender (*zawierzenie*), and hope (*nadzieja*)

**Thematic center: God alone.** Every prayer orbits around God the Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Spirit. Scripture is the primary devotional fuel. No secondary devotions, no saints' intercession, no Marian elements. The listener encounters God directly through His Word and through the prayer companion's voice.

# Style
Polish Catholic guided morning prayer: warm, reverent, Scripture-saturated, emotionally soothing, Christocentric, with a contemplative depth rooted in God's Word.

# Tone
Warm, gentle, hopeful, reverent, intimate, reassuring, unhurried, trusting, reflective, consoling — the quiet strength of a man who has learned to rest in God.

# Writing Style
- **Register**: Formal yet tender Polish prayer register. Use consistent capitalized pronouns when addressing God (Ty, Ciebie, Twój, Tobie, Ci) and reverential vocatives (Panie, Boże, Ojcze). For the self, use humble first-person plural "my" (we) as the default to include the narrator and listener as one praying community. When shifting to singular "ja" (I) for moments of personal confession, **always use masculine forms**: *oddałem* (not *oddałam*), *stanąłem* (not *stanęłam*), *jestem wdzięczny* (not *wdzięczna*).
- **Dialogue handling**: There is no human dialogue. God's voice appears only through directly quoted Scripture, seamlessly embedded into the prayer. Introduce Scripture with "Ty sam powiedziałeś…" (You Yourself said…), "jak mówi Twoje Słowo…" (as Your Word says…), or simply by quoting the verse with a slight pause.
- **Level of formality**: High formality but never stiff. The prayer feels like a flowing, intimate conversation with God while maintaining deep reverence — the tone of a man kneeling before his Creator, speaking with honesty and awe.
- **Density of imagery**: Moderate. Use gentle nature/light imagery (morning light — *poranne światło*, a path — *droga*, a guiding hand — *dłoń*, shelter — *schronienie*, healing — *uzdrowienie*). Never abstract or poetic for its own sake. Every image serves reassurance and points to God's character.
- **Use of internal thought**: The prayer externalizes internal thought by speaking it directly to God ("Wyznaję Ci, Panie, że moje serce jest niespokojne…"), so the listener's own unspoken fears are voiced and surrendered aloud.

# ★ CORE BACKBONE: The Three-Beat Block (most important structural rule)
This is the single most defining feature of the Korean source channel and must drive the Polish scripts too. The body of the prayer is built by **stacking these blocks**. Each block follows three beats:
1. **Scripture (Słowo)** — quote one Bible verse, near-verbatim in the Polish Catholic register (Biblia Tysiąclecia), introduced naturally ("Ty sam powiedziałeś…", "Twoje Słowo mówi…") with no academic citation.
2. **Thanksgiving on that truth (dziękczynienie)** — give thanks for the very truth just quoted: "Dziękuję Ci, Panie, za tę obietnicę…" / "Dzięki Ci składam za tę łaskę…"
3. **Petition applied to today (prośba)** — ask for that promise to work in today's life, ending with reverent request forms: "…prowadź nas Twoim Słowem.", "…strzeż naszych serc, Panie.", "…umacniaj nas dziś."

Each block covers ONE life-theme (peace → fear/trust → family → health/healing → work → relationships → provision → guidance → mercy), then the next verse opens the next block. Keep each block self-contained so it is a complete "dose" of comfort + reassurance. This stacking is what creates the meditative, cyclical 1-hour flow.

# Sentence Structure
- **Length patterns**: Long, flowing sentences joined by conjunctions (i, a, gdyż, bo, aby, choć, nawet gdy) and relative pronouns (który, która, które). One "sentence" often spans 3–5 lines when transcribed — a meditative, breathing rhythm for audio.
- **Rhythm and punctuation**: Pauses created by sub-clauses and the vocative "Panie"/"Boże" as breath markers. Standard Polish punctuation; ellipses (…) may mark meditative pauses. The prayer flows as an unbroken stream of address.
- **Paragraphing**: Breaks at major thematic shifts. Within paragraphs, repetition of a key phrase unifies the section (cycling "nawet gdy… Ty, Panie…").

# POV / Narrator Voice
- **Person**: 1st person plural ("my"/"nas"/"nasze") is default; shifts to 1st person singular ("ja"/"moje") for personal confession — **always masculine**. Pick ONE dominant register per script and keep it consistent, with only occasional shifts.
- **Addressing God**: Direct address only (Panie, Boże, Ojcze, Jezu, Duchu Święty). No third-person storytelling about God. Characters are God and us — no intermediaries.
- **Internal-vs-external balance**: Almost entirely internal and spoken aloud; only name life situations (health, family, work) to ground the prayer.
- **Gender consistency**: The narrator is a man praying — *stanąłem* not *stanęłam*, *jestem wdzięczny* not *wdzięczna*. Consistent in every script without exception.

# Hook Pattern
First 100–200 words establish gratitude, acknowledge the new day as God's gift, set a tone of surrender:
0. **Sign of the Cross (MANDATORY, always the first line):** „W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen." — on its own line before any other word. This is the Catholic marker that opens every script.
1. Tender, praise-filled vocative (Wszechmogący Boże / Wierny Ojcze / Panie Jezu Chryste).
2. Thank Him for the new morning and protection through the night.
3. Express the desire to entrust the day entirely to Him.
4. Optionally introduce the anchor Scripture (often Iz 41:10).

**Model openings:**
- „W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen. Wszechmogący i wierny Boże, dziękujemy Ci za ten nowy dzień, który w swojej łaskawości nam dajesz. Czuwałeś nad nami w ciemności nocy i budzisz nas do kolejnego poranka. Wyznajemy z wiarą, że ten dzień należy do Ciebie, i oddajemy go w Twoje ręce — każdą godzinę, każdą chwilę, każdy krok."
- „W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen. Panie Jezu Chryste, stoję przed Tobą w ciszy tego poranka z sercem pełnym wdzięczności. Stanąłem tu nie dlatego, że jestem silny, ale dlatego, że Ty jesteś wierny. Oddaję Ci ten dzień i proszę — prowadź mnie Twoim Słowem."

# Pacing & Retention Tactics
- **Cyclical structure**: Revisit themes (peace, trust, protection, family, mercy) in waves, each deepening surrender or adding a Scripture promise.
- **Scripture anchoring**: Every 200–400 words, quote a verse directly, then a personal response („Dziękuję Ci za tę obietnicę, Panie"). Resets attention; gives an emotional peak. Scripture is the backbone.
- **Life-area scanning**: Move through heart → body → family → workplace → decisions → relationships, keeping it relatable.
- **Mini-twists of reversal**: „nawet gdy X, Ty, Panie, Y" (e.g., „nawet gdy droga wydaje się zamknięta, Ty już otwierasz przede mną inną bramę") converts worry into hope.
- **Sentence-length variation**: Mostly long, but insert short confessions („Panie, oddaję Ci ten dzień. Cały należy do Ciebie.") as rest points.

# ★ Length / 1-hour Runtime Technique
The Korean source reaches ~1 hour mainly by (a) stacking many three-beat blocks across all life-areas, and (b) **re-cycling the entire body once more after the first full pass** before the close. To extend a Polish script: prefer adding NEW three-beat blocks with fresh verses from the bank; if a full re-cycle is used for runtime, place it AFTER the first complete arc and BEFORE the closing, and keep only ONE closing/Amen at the very end.

# Emotional Arc
1. Gratitude & opening surrender (calm, safe)
2. Acknowledgment of worry, fear, burdens (tension)
3. Cry for help + Scripture promises (rising hope — „Nie lękaj się, bo Ja jestem z tobą")
4. Resting in God's faithfulness / healing words (peak reassurance — mercy, covenant)
5. Intercession for family, health, work, others (gentle outward expansion)
6. Reaffirmation of trust and commitment (return to calm)
7. Blessing and gentle close through Christ (lingering peace)
Peaks usually fall on the first major promise (often Iz 41:10) and again near the midpoint when God's faithfulness is declared.

# Closing Pattern
1. Final entrustment: „Panie, oddaję ten dzień całkowicie w Twoje ręce."
2. Blessing cluster: „Strzeż naszych rodzin, naszej pracy, naszego zdrowia i naszego pokoju. Niech Twoje Słowo będzie lampą dla naszych stóp i światłem na naszej ścieżce."
3. Gentle commissioning: „Pozwól nam, Panie, rozpocząć ten dzień w dziękczynieniu i zakończyć go w Twoim pokoju."
4. Formal liturgical ending: „Wszystkie nasze modlitwy i pragnienia serc składamy przed Tobą, przez Chrystusa, Pana naszego. Amen."
5. Optional closing frame (Sign of the Cross): „W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen."

# Narrative Structure (three-movement devotional)
- **Act 1 (Opening)** — Sign of the Cross, praise, gratitude, presentation of the day. (~10–15%)
- **Act 2 (Petition & Scripture engagement)** — Core: stacked three-beat blocks cycling through personal, familial, communal concerns, each opened by a transition („Panie, powierzamy Ci dziś nasze rodziny…", „Boże, oddajemy w Twoje ręce naszą pracę…") and a verse from the bank. (~70–80%)
- **Act 3 (Closing)** — Final surrender, benediction, Amen. (~10%)
Transitions refocus with "Panie" or "Boże wierny". Overall effect: a slow, guided ascent from worry to peace.

# Do
- **Always** open every script with „W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen." as the very first line.
- Build the body as stacked **three-beat blocks** (Słowo → dziękczynienie → prośba).
- Use the **fixed Polish Scripture bank**; embed 8–12 verses per full-length script, introduced naturally.
- Use "my" (we) inclusively (or "ja" consistently masculine) so narrator and listener pray as one.
- Use **masculine grammatical forms** for all first-person singular expressions without exception.
- Capitalize all divine pronouns (Ty, Ciebie, Twój, Tobie, Ci).
- Keep sentence flow long and meditative, using subordinate clauses and participial phrases.
- Move from personal inner fears → family → workplace/society → final surrender.
- Reassure repeatedly with the „nawet gdy… Ty, Panie…" pattern.
- Keep the prayer entirely **Christocentric**; blessing/abundance motifs only grace-framed.
- End every script with „Przez Chrystusa, Pana naszego. Amen." (optionally followed by the Sign of the Cross).
- Maintain unwavering reverence; never address God casually.

# Don't
- Do NOT use second-person commands toward the listener („musisz").
- Do NOT introduce complex theology or doctrinal debates.
- Do NOT use slang, colloquial Polish, or casual register.
- Do NOT leave a fear or anxiety unresolved; meet every negative emotion with Scripture-based hope before moving on.
- Do NOT address God without reverence; always capitalize divine pronouns, always use vocatives.
- Do NOT exclude the listener's family or community; always broaden from "I" to "we."
- Do NOT end abruptly; always land softly with a blessing and Amen.
- Do NOT mention specific dates, political issues/parties, or divisive topics.
- Do NOT incorporate folk superstition, prosperity-gospel claims, New Age, or esoteric content.
- Do NOT include Marian devotion, saints' intercession, rosary, or any devotion outside the soul's direct relationship with God through Christ — apart from the Sign of the Cross frame.
- Do NOT use feminine grammatical forms for the narrator (*oddałam, stanęłam, wdzięczna*) — always *oddałem, stanąłem, wdzięczny*.
- Do NOT use a triumphalist tone; the spirit is quiet trust and steady faith.
