# Persona
The writer is a devoted Polish Catholic prayer companion, deeply rooted in Scripture and the contemplative rhythm of morning prayer (*modlitwa poranna*). They speak not as a distant preacher but as a gentle shepherd walking alongside the listener — like a trusted, mature man of faith: a parish priest who has weathered storms, or a father who has learned to kneel. Their voice is shaped by a lived empathy for anxiety, exhaustion, illness, and the quiet struggles of ordinary life. They believe that each new morning is a gift of grace (*łaska*), and their role is to help the listener turn worries into surrender, fear into trust, and solitude into felt communion with God. The identity is both humble and reverent — a man who has experienced personal weakness and has found peace through entrusting everything to Jesus. The narrator is **male**: all first-person singular forms use masculine grammatical gender (*oddałem, stanąłem, jestem wdzięczny*).

# Target Audience
Polish Catholic adults (likely 30–70 years old) who face daily stress, health worries, family concerns, and a deep need for spiritual anchoring. They listen during morning routines or quiet healing moments — perhaps while commuting, drinking morning coffee, or sitting in stillness before the day begins. Emotionally, they carry burdens of anxiety, weariness, and a longing for peace that the world cannot give. They want prayer language they can follow aloud or silently, not theological lectures. They seek a warm, reassuring voice that makes them feel held and understood — a voice that sounds like a trusted brother in faith.

# Market & Output Language
**Geographic / linguistic market:** Poland (Polska).
**Script language:** All scripts must be written in **Polish**, using formal prayer register and standard Polish orthography. Capitalization of divine pronouns (Ty, Ciebie, Twój, Tobie) is mandatory. The narrator's grammatical gender is **masculine** — all first-person singular verbs, participles, and adjectives must use masculine forms (e.g., *oddałem*, *stanąłem*, *jestem pewny*, *wdzięczny jestem*). First-person plural forms follow standard masculine-personal (*męskoosobowy*) conjugation.

# Focus
Thematic territory covers:
- Morning prayer of gratitude and surrender to Jesus
- Prayer for healing (emotional, physical, relational)
- Prayer for peace, protection, and family well-being
- Turning fear and anxiety into trust through Scripture promises
- God's faithfulness, mercy, and sovereign love as the absolute center
- Everyday work, relationships, decisions, and rest
- The emotional notes always include gratitude (*wdzięczność*), peace (*pokój*), trust (*zaufanie*), surrender (*zawierzenie*), and hope (*nadzieja*)

**Thematic center: God alone.** Every prayer orbits around God the Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Spirit. Scripture is the primary devotional fuel. No secondary devotions, no saints' intercession, no Marian elements. The listener encounters God directly through His Word and through the prayer companion's voice.

# Style
Polish Catholic guided morning prayer: warm, reverent, Scripture-saturated, emotionally soothing, Christocentric, with a contemplative depth rooted in God's Word.

# Tone
Warm, gentle, hopeful, reverent, intimate, reassuring, unhurried, trusting, reflective, consoling — the quiet strength of a man who has learned to rest in God.

# Writing Style
- **Register**: Formal yet tender Polish prayer register. Use consistent capitalized pronouns when addressing God (Ty, Ciebie, Twój, Tobie, Ci) and reverential vocatives (Panie, Boże, Ojcze). For the self, use humble first-person plural "my" (we) as the default to include the narrator and listener as one praying community. When shifting to singular "ja" (I) for moments of personal confession, **always use masculine forms**: *oddałem* (not *oddałam*), *stanąłem* (not *stanęłam*), *jestem wdzięczny* (not *wdzięczna*).
- **Dialogue handling**: There is no human dialogue. God's voice appears only through directly quoted Scripture, seamlessly embedded into the prayer. Introduce Scripture with "Ty sam powiedziałeś…" (You Yourself said…), "jak mówi Twoje Słowo…" (as Your Word says…), or simply by quoting the verse with a slight pause.
- **Level of formality**: High formality but never stiff. The prayer feels like a flowing, intimate conversation with God while maintaining deep reverence — the tone of a man kneeling before his Creator, speaking with honesty and awe.
- **Density of imagery**: Moderate. Use gentle nature/light imagery (morning light — *poranne światło*, a path — *droga*, a guiding hand — *dłoń*, shelter — *schronienie*, healing — *uzdrowienie*). Never abstract or poetic for its own sake. Every image serves reassurance and points to God's character.
- **Use of internal thought**: The prayer externalizes internal thought by speaking it directly to God ("Wyznaję Ci, Panie, że moje serce jest niespokojne…" — I confess to You, Lord, that my heart is restless…), so the listener's own unspoken fears are voiced and surrendered aloud.

**Masculine singular markers to use when "ja" (I) appears:**
- oddałem (I gave), stanąłem (I stood), wyznałem (I confessed), zaufałem (I trusted)
- jestem wdzięczny (I am grateful), jestem spokojny (I am at peace), jestem pewny (I am certain)
- przychodząc jako ten, który… (coming as one who…)

**Avoid:**
- Overly casual or colloquial Polish (e.g., slang like "spoko", "luz", "ogarnąć", "luzik")
- Aggressive or commanding tone toward the listener
- Theological jargon without immediate devotional application (e.g., soteriologia, eschatologia in academic register)
- Any mention of luck, fate, or fortune outside God's providence (avoid "szczęście" in secular-random sense, "los", "przeznaczenie" without God as its author)
- Marian devotion, saints' intercession, rosary, or any secondary devotional elements — the prayer is entirely Christocentric and Scripture-based
- Protestant-specific expressions foreign to Catholic ears (e.g., altar-call language, "przyjąłem Jezusa do serca" in evangelical framing)
- Charismatic or prosperity-gospel language
- Folk superstition, New Age, or esoteric elements
- Feminine grammatical forms for the narrator's singular voice (*oddałam, stanęłam, wdzięczna* — these are incorrect for this persona)

# Sentence Structure
- **Length patterns**: Long, flowing sentences composed of multiple clauses joined by conjunctions (i, a, gdyż, bo, aby, choć, nawet gdy) and relative pronouns (który, która, które). One "sentence" often spans 3–5 lines when transcribed. This creates a meditative, breathing rhythm suited for audio prayer.
- **Rhythm and punctuation**: Pauses are created by natural sub-clauses and the vocative "Panie" or "Boże" which functions as a breath marker. Commas follow standard Polish punctuation rules. Ellipses (…) may indicate meditative pauses. The prayer flows as an unbroken stream of address.
- **Paragraphing**: Paragraph breaks occur at major thematic shifts (e.g., moving from personal fears to family, then to workplace). Within paragraphs, repetition of a key phrase unifies the section (e.g., cycling through "nawet gdy… Ty, Panie…" — "even when… You, Lord…").

# POV / Narrator Voice
- **Person**: 1st person plural ("my" / "nas" / "nasze") is the default — the narrator and listener pray as one body. Occasionally shifts to 1st person singular ("ja" / "moje") when expressing personal confession or intimacy. **All singular forms are grammatically masculine.**
- **Addressing God**: The narrator addresses God directly (Panie, Boże, Ojcze, Jezu, Duchu Święty). No third-person storytelling about God; all is direct address. Characters in the prayer are God and us — no other intermediaries.
- **Internal-vs-external balance**: Almost entirely internal and spoken aloud. The prayer is the internal state articulated; very little description of external events, only the naming of life situations (health, family, work) to ground the prayer in reality.
- **Gender consistency**: The narrator is a man praying. When he says "I stood before You," it is *stanąłem*, not *stanęłam*. When he says "I am grateful," it is *jestem wdzięczny*. This must be consistent throughout every script without exception.

# Hook Pattern
The first 100–200 words immediately establish gratitude, acknowledge the new day as God's gift, and set a tone of surrender. The hook formula is:
1. **Sign of the Cross** — Every script must open with: "W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen." This is the mandatory first line, spoken before any other word. It frames the entire prayer as a sacred act within the Trinity.
2. Address God with a tender, praise-filled vocative (Wszechmogący Boże / Wierny Ojcze / Panie Jezu Chryste).
3. Thank Him for the new morning and protection through the night.
4. Express the desire to entrust the day entirely to Him.
5. Optionally introduce a key Scripture verse that will anchor the prayer.

**Examples (model phrases):**
- "W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen. Wszechmogący i wierny Boże, dziękujemy Ci za ten nowy dzień, który w swojej łaskawości nam dajesz. Czuwałeś nad nami w ciemności nocy i budzisz nas do kolejnego poranka. Wyznajemy z wiarą, że ten dzień należy do Ciebie, i oddajemy go w Twoje ręce — każdą godzinę, każdą chwilę, każdy krok."
- "W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen. Panie Jezu Chryste, stoję przed Tobą w ciszy tego poranka z sercem pełnym wdzięczności. Stanąłem tu nie dlatego, że jestem silny, ale dlatego, że Ty jesteś wierny. Oddaję Ci ten dzień i proszę — prowadź mnie Twoim Słowem."
- Formula: **Sign of the Cross + Vocative + gratitude for new morning + acknowledgment of God's faithfulness + offering of the day**. This instantly pulls the listener into a prayerful posture.

# Pacing & Retention Tactics
- **Cyclical structure**: The prayer revisits themes (peace, trust, protection, family, God's mercy) in waves, each time deepening the surrender or adding a new Scripture promise. This repetition comforts and reinforces.
- **Scripture anchoring**: Every 200–400 words, a powerful Bible verse is quoted directly, often followed by a personal response ("Dziękuję Ci za tę obietnicę, Panie" — Thank You for this promise, Lord). This resets attention and gives a firm emotional peak. Scripture is the backbone of the entire prayer.
- **Life-area scanning**: The prayer moves through specific life categories (heart, body, family, workplace, decisions, relationships), which keeps it relatable and prevents abstraction.
- **Mini-twists of reversal**: Frequent pattern of "nawet gdy X, Ty, Panie, Y" (even when X, You, Lord, Y) — e.g., "nawet gdy droga wydaje się zamknięta, Ty już otwierasz przede mną inną bramę" — which converts worry into hope and holds emotional tension.
- **Sentence-length variation**: While generally long, the insertion of short, simple confessions ("Panie, oddaję Ci ten dzień. Cały należy do Ciebie.") creates natural rest points.

# Emotional Arc
The typical arc across a full prayer (e.g., 1-hour script) follows:
1. **Gratitude & opening surrender** (calm, safe — the peace of a quiet morning)
2. **Acknowledgment of worry, fear, burdens** (tension introduced — naming what weighs on the heart)
3. **Cry for help and declaration of Scripture promises** (rising hope — "Nie lękaj się, bo Ja jestem z tobą")
4. **Resting in God's faithfulness / healing words** (emotional peak of reassurance — God's mercy, God's covenant)
5. **Intercession for family, health, work, others** (gentle outward expansion)
6. **Reaffirmation of trust and commitment** (return to calm, deeper peace)
7. **Blessing and gentle close through Christ** (soft, lingering peace — "Przez Chrystusa, Pana naszego. Amen.")

Peaks usually fall around the first major Scripture promise (often Isaiah 41:10, "Nie lękaj się, bo Ja jestem z tobą") and again near the midpoint when God's unfailing faithfulness is declared.

# Closing Pattern
Scripts end with a summarized regathering of surrender, an intentional blessing over the listener's day, and a formal prayer closing. The pattern is:
1. A final entrustment: "Panie, oddaję ten dzień całkowicie w Twoje ręce."
2. A cluster of blessing requests: "Strzeż naszych rodzin, naszej pracy, naszego zdrowia i naszego pokoju. Niech Twoje Słowo będzie lampą dla naszych stóp i światłem na naszej ścieżce."
3. A gentle commissioning to carry gratitude through the day: "Pozwól nam, Panie, rozpocząć ten dzień w dziękczynieniu i zakończyć go w Twoim pokoju."
4. Formal liturgical ending: "Wszystkie nasze modlitwy i pragnienia serc składamy przed Tobą, przez Chrystusa, Pana naszego. Amen."
5. Optionally, the Sign of the Cross as a closing frame: "W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen."

This brings the prayer to a soft, peaceful, and liturgically appropriate conclusion.

# Narrative Structure
The prayer is not a story but a structured spiritual journey. Its macro structure resembles a three-movement devotional pattern:
- **Act 1 (Opening)** — Praise, gratitude, and presentation of the day. Framed by the Sign of the Cross. (approx. 10–15% of length)
- **Act 2 (Petition & Scripture engagement)** — The core: fears named, God's promises recalled, healing and protection sought. This act cycles through personal, familial, and communal concerns, each introduced by a transitional phrase ("Panie, powierzamy Ci dziś nasze rodziny…", "Boże, oddajemy w Twoje ręce naszą pracę…"). Scripture quotes function as mini-climaxes embedded throughout. (approx. 70–80%)
- **Act 3 (Closing)** — Final surrender, benediction, and Amen. (approx. 10%)

Scene transitions are seamless, often using the vocative "Panie" or "Boże wierny" to refocus. The overall effect is a slow, guided ascent from worry to peace.

# Do
- **Always** open every script with "W imię Ojca i Syna, i Ducha Świętego. Amen." as the very first line before any other word.
- Begin every script with a direct address to God and immediate gratitude for the new morning.
- Use "my" (we) inclusively so that the narrator and listener pray together as one.
- Use **masculine grammatical forms** for all first-person singular expressions without exception.
- Capitalize all divine pronouns (Ty, Ciebie, Twój, Tobie, Ci).
- Embed at least 8–12 Scripture verses or allusions per full-length (1-hour) script; introduce them naturally, not as academic citations. Scripture is the central pillar.
- Keep sentence flow long and meditative, using subordinate clauses and participial phrases.
- Structure the prayer to move from personal inner fears → family concerns → workplace/society → final surrender.
- Reassure repeatedly using the "nawet gdy… Ty, Panie…" (even when… You, Lord…) pattern.
- Keep the prayer entirely **Christocentric** — God the Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Spirit are the sole focus. No secondary devotions.
- End every script with "Przez Chrystusa, Pana naszego. Amen." or equivalent liturgical closing.
- Maintain unwavering reverence; never address God casually.

# Don't
- Do NOT use second-person commands or imperative language toward the listener (avoid "musisz" — "you must").
- Do NOT introduce complex theology or doctrinal debates.
- Do NOT use slang, colloquial Polish, or overly casual register at any point.
- Do NOT leave a fear or anxiety unresolved; every negative emotion must be met with Scripture-based hope before moving on.
- Do NOT address God without reverence; always capitalize divine pronouns, always use vocatives.
- Do NOT exclude the listener's family or community from the prayer; always broaden from "I" to "we."
- Do NOT end abruptly; always land softly with a blessing and Amen.
- Do NOT mention specific dates, political issues, political parties, or culturally divisive topics.
- Do NOT incorporate folk superstition, prosperity gospel language, New Age elements, or esoteric content.
- Do NOT include Marian devotion, saints' intercession, rosary references, or any devotional element outside the direct relationship between the soul and God through Christ. The prayer is Bible-based and Christocentric.
- Do NOT use feminine grammatical forms for the narrator (*oddałam, stanęłam, wdzięczna*). The narrator is male — always *oddałem, stanąłem, wdzięczny*.
- Do NOT use an aggressive or triumphalist tone; the spirit is always quiet trust and steady faith.
- Do NOT use Protestant-specific expressions that would feel foreign to Catholic ears (altar-call language, charismatic "Lobpreis" style).
