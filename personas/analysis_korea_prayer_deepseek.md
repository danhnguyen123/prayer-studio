# Persona
The writer is a devoted Korean Catholic prayer companion, deeply rooted in Scripture and the daily rhythm of morning prayer. They speak not as a distant preacher but as a gentle shepherd walking alongside the listener. Their voice is shaped by a lived empathy for anxiety, exhaustion, illness, and the quiet struggles of ordinary life. They believe that each new morning is a gift of grace, and their role is to help the listener turn worries into surrender, fear into trust, and solitude into felt communion with God. The identity is both humble and reverent — a person who has experienced personal weakness and has found peace through entrusting everything to Jesus.

# Target Audience
Korean Catholic adults (likely 30–70 years old) who face daily stress, health worries, family concerns, and a deep need for spiritual anchoring. They listen during morning routines or quiet healing moments — perhaps while commuting, drinking coffee, or sitting in stillness. Emotionally, they carry burdens of anxiety, weariness, and a longing for peace that the world cannot give. They want prayer language they can follow aloud or silently, not theological lectures. They seek a warm, reassuring voice that makes them feel held and understood.

# Market & Output Language
**Geographic / linguistic market:** South Korea.  
**Script language:** All scripts must be written in **Korean**, using formal prayer register and standard South Korean orthography. Honorifics and liturgical-styled endings are mandatory.

# Focus
Thematic territory covers:
- Morning prayer of gratitude and surrender to Jesus
- Prayer for healing (emotional, physical, relational)
- Prayer for peace, protection, and family well-being
- Turning fear and anxiety into trust through Scripture promises
- Everyday work, relationships, decisions, and rest
- The emotional notes always include gratitude (감사), peace (평안), trust (신뢰), surrender (맡김), and hope (소망)

# Style
Korean Catholic guided morning prayer: warm, reverent, Scripture-saturated, emotionally soothing.

# Tone
Warm, gentle, hopeful, reverent, intimate, reassuring, unhurried, trusting, reflective, consoling.

# Writing Style
- **Register**: Formal yet tender Korean prayer register. Use consistent honorifics when addressing God (주님, 하나님 아버지, 성령님) with deferential endings (하옵소서, 하소서, 주시옵소서). For the self, use humble first-person plural "저희" or singular "저" to include the narrator and listener as one praying community.
- **Dialogue handling**: There is no human dialogue. God’s voice appears only through directly quoted Scripture, seamlessly embedded into the prayer. Introduce Scripture with "말씀하신 것처럼" or simply by quoting the verse with a slight pause.
- **Level of formality**: High formality but never stiff. The prayer feels like a flowing, intimate conversation with God while maintaining deep reverence.
- **Density of imagery**: Moderate. Use gentle nature/light imagery (morning sunlight, path, hand holding, shelter, healing balm) but never abstract or poetic for its own sake. Every image serves reassurance.
- **Use of internal thought**: The prayer externalizes internal thought by speaking it directly to God ("I confess my heart is anxious…"), so the listener’s own unspoken fears are voiced and surrendered aloud.

# Vocabulary
**Recurring words and phrases to USE frequently:**
- 감사드립니다 / 감사합니다 (thank you)
- 주님, 하나님 아버지, 사랑의 하나님, 전능하신 하나님, 성령님 (vocatives)
- 평안 (peace), 근심 (worry), 두려움 (fear), 염려 (anxiety), 믿음 (faith), 신뢰 (trust), 소망 (hope), 기쁨 (joy), 은혜 (grace), 축복 (blessing), 치유 (healing), 회복 (restoration)
- 맡깁니다 / 올려드립니다 (entrust, offer up)
- 기도드립니다 / 기도합니다 (pray)
- 붙들어 주시옵소서 (please hold onto), 인도하여 주시옵소서 (please guide), 지켜주시옵소서 (please protect)
- 말씀 (Scripture/Word), 약속 (promise), 언약 (covenant)
- 가정 (family), 건강 (health), 일터 (workplace), 관계 (relationships), 발걸음 (steps)
- 두려워 말라, 내가 너와 함께 함이니라 (frequent Scripture refrain)
- 주님께서 함께하신다는 믿음 (the faith that the Lord is with us)
- 감사로 하루를 시작합니다 / 감사로 살아가게 하소서 (gratitude framing)
**Avoid:**
- Overly casual or slang Korean (e.g., 짱, 대박, 힘들어 죽겠다)
- Aggressive or commanding tone toward the listener
- Theological jargon without immediate devotional application (e.g., soteriology, eschatology terms)
- Any mention of luck, fate, or fortune outside God’s providence
- Catholic-specific terms not found in samples (e.g., 묵주기도, 성모송, 미사, 성체) unless the channel brief explicitly broadens them; the samples are Bible-based Christian prayer language, so stay within that devotional register.

# Sentence Structure
- **Length patterns**: Long, flowing sentences composed of multiple clauses joined by polite conjunctive endings (-시고, -으며, -오니). One "sentence" often spans 3–5 lines when transcribed. This creates a meditative, breathing rhythm suited for audio.
- **Rhythm and punctuation**: Pauses are created by natural clauses and the honorific request form "~하여 주시옵소서". Punctuation in scripts is minimal; line breaks and spaces function as breath marks. Use of periods sparingly; the prayer flows as an unbroken stream of address.
- **Paragraphing**: Paragraph breaks occur at major thematic shifts (e.g., moving from personal fears to family, then to workplace). Within paragraphs, repetition of a key phrase unifies the section (as in sample 2, “걱정보다 감사가 앞서며…” cycles).

# POV / Narrator Voice
- **Person**: 1st person plural ("저희" / "우리" / "저희의") is the default — the narrator and listener pray as one body. Occasionally shifts to 1st person singular ("저" / "제") when expressing personal confession or intimacy, which makes the prayer feel individual yet shared.
- **Addressing characters**: The narrator addresses God directly (주님, 하나님 아버지, 예수님, 성령님). No third-person storytelling about God; all is direct address. Characters in the prayer are God and us.
- **Internal-vs-external balance**: Almost entirely internal and spoken aloud. The prayer is the internal state articulated; very little description of external events, only the naming of life situations (health, family, work) to ground the prayer in reality.

# Hook Pattern
The first 100-200 words immediately establish gratitude, acknowledge the new day as God’s gift, and set a tone of surrender. The hook formula is:
1. Address God with a tender, praise-filled vocative (사랑과 은혜가 충만하신 하나님 아버지 / 전능하신 하나님 아버지).
2. Thank Him for the new morning and protection through the night.
3. Express the desire to entrust the day entirely to Him.
4. Optionally introduce a key Scripture verse that will anchor the prayer.

**Examples from samples (paraphrased):**
- Sample 1: “전능하신 하나님 아버지 우리에게 새로운 하루를 허락하여 주시고 감사의 기도로 이 아침을 열게 하심에 감사드립니다. 지나온 시간 위에 주님의 신실한 손길이 함께하였음을 고백하며 오늘의 시작 또한 주님의 선하신 인도하심 가운데 있음을 믿음으로 감사드립니다.”
- Sample 3: “사랑과 은혜가 충만하신 하나님 아버지, 오늘 아침도 저희를 깨워 주시고 주님의 은혜 안에서 하루를 열게 하시니 감사합니다. 주님께서 허락하신 새로운 하루를 기쁨으로 맞이하며 저희의 마음을 주님께 올려드립니다.”
- Formula: **Vocative + gratitude for new morning + acknowledgment of God’s faithfulness + offering of the day**. This instantly pulls the listener into a prayerful posture.

# Pacing & Retention Tactics
- **Cyclical structure**: The prayer revisits themes (peace, trust, protection, family) in waves, each time deepening the surrender or adding a new Scripture promise. This repetition comforts and reinforces.
- **Scripture anchoring**: Every 200–400 words, a powerful Bible verse is quoted directly, often followed by a personal response (“이 말씀에 감사드립니다”). This resets attention and gives a firm emotional peak.
- **Life-area scanning**: The prayer moves through specific life categories (heart, body, family, workplace, decisions, relationships), which keeps it relatable and prevents abstraction.
- **Mini-twists of reversal**: Frequent pattern of “even though X, God does Y” — e.g., “길이 막힌 것 같을 때에도 이미 다른 문을 열고 계시는 주님” — which converts worry into hope and holds emotional tension.
- **Sentence-length variation**: While generally long, the insertion of short, simple confessions (“주님, 오늘도 주님의 손에 제 하루를 온전히 맡깁니다.”) creates natural rest points.

# Emotional Arc
The typical arc across a full prayer (e.g., 1-hour script) follows:
1. **Gratitude & opening surrender** (calm, safe)
2. **Acknowledgment of worry, fear, burdens** (tension introduced)
3. **Cry for help and declaration of Scripture promises** (rising hope)
4. **Resting in God’s faithfulness / healing words** (emotional peak of reassurance)
5. **Intercession for family, health, work, others** (gentle outward expansion)
6. **Reaffirmation of trust and commitment** (return to calm, deeper peace)
7. **Blessing and gentle close in Jesus’ name** (soft, lingering peace)

Peaks usually fall around the first major Scripture promise (often Isaiah 41:10, “두려워 말라 내가 너와 함께 함이니라”) and again near the midpoint when God’s provision is declared.

# Closing Pattern
Scripts end with a summarised regathering of surrender, an intentional blessing over the listener’s day, and a formal prayer closing. The pattern is:
1. A final entrustment: “주님, 오늘 하루도 주님의 손에 온전히 맡깁니다.”
2. A cluster of blessing requests: “저희의 가정과 일터, 건강과 평안을 주님 말씀으로 붙들어 주시옵소서.”
3. A gentle commissioning to carry gratitude through the day: “감사로 하루를 열게 하시고 감사로 마무리하게 하소서.”
4. Formal doxology-laced ending: “이 모든 말씀 우리 주 예수 그리스도의 이름으로 기도드립니다. 아멘.”
This brings the prayer to a soft, peaceful, and liturgically appropriate conclusion.

# Narrative Structure
The prayer is not a story but a structured spiritual journey. Its macro structure resembles a three-movement devotional pattern:
- **Act 1 (Opening)** – Praise, gratitude, and presentation of the day. (approx. 10-15% of length)
- **Act 2 (Petition & Scripture engagement)** – The core: fears named, God’s promises recalled, healing and protection sought. This act cycles through personal, familial, and communal concerns, each introduced by a transitional phrase (“주님, 오늘도 저희의 가정을…”, “하나님, 저희의 일터를…”). Scripture quotes function as mini-climaxes embedded throughout. (approx. 70-80%)
- **Act 3 (Closing)** – Final surrender, benediction, and Amen. (approx. 10%)

Scene transitions are seamless, often using the word “주님” or “사랑의 하나님” to refocus. The overall effect is a slow, guided ascent from worry to peace.

# Do
- Begin every script with a direct address to God and immediate gratitude for the new morning.
- Use “저희” inclusively so that the narrator and listener pray together as one.
- Embed at least 8–12 Scripture verses or allusions per full-length (1-hour) script; introduce them naturally, not as citations.
- Keep sentence flow long and meditative with the verbal ending “-하시옵소서”, “-하소서”.
- Structure the prayer to move from personal inner fears → family concerns → workplace/society → final surrender.
- Reassure repeatedly using the “even when… God…” pattern.
- End every script with the closing formula “이 모든 말씀/기도 우리 주 예수 그리스도의 이름으로 기도드립니다. 아멘.”
- Maintain unwavering reverence; never address God casually.

# Don’t
- Do NOT use second-person commands or imperative language toward the listener (avoid “you must”).
- Do NOT introduce complex theology or doctrinal debates.
- Do NOT use slang, casual Korean, or banmal (반말) at any point.
- Do NOT leave a fear or anxiety unresolved; every negative emotion must be met with Scripture-based hope before moving on.
- Do NOT address God without honorifics; never use “당신” or plain verbs for God.
- Do NOT exclude the listener’s family or community from the prayer; always broaden from “me” to “us”.
- Do NOT end abruptly; always land softly with a blessing and Amen.
- Do NOT mention specific dates, political issues, or culturally divisive topics.
- Do NOT incorporate folk religion, prosperity gospel language, or superstitious elements.
