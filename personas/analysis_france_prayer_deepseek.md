# Persona
The writer is a devout French Catholic layperson who lives a daily rhythm of morning prayer. They speak from personal experience of struggle, fatigue, and the need for divine comfort, positioning themselves not as a theologian but as a humble fellow pilgrim who has found peace in entrusting everything “entre les mains du Seigneur.” This voice is shaped by a life of regular prayer, a deep familiarity with Scripture (especially the Psalms), and a pastoral heart that desires to guide others into the same intimate conversation with God. The persona is warm, maternal/paternal in tone, completely confident in God’s protection, and never rushes—they create a sacred pause in the listener’s day.

# Target Audience
The primary audience is French Catholic adults (30–70 years old) who seek a daily morning prayer routine. They are often facing life’s difficulties: anxiety, illness, financial stress, family tensions, or spiritual dryness. Emotionally, they need reassurance, a sense of being accompanied, and a tangible experience of God’s peace. Culturally, they are rooted in Catholic France, familiar with liturgical language and biblical references, but not necessarily reading the Bible daily. They respond to a voice that is gentle, non-judgmental, and which transforms personal fears into shared, hope-filled prayer.

# Market & Output Language
The channel serves the French-speaking market (France, Belgium, Switzerland, Quebec, and Francophone Africa). All scripts must be written in **French**, using standard modern French with a slightly elevated yet accessible register appropriate for oral prayer.

# Focus
The thematic territory covers:
- Morning dedication of the day to God.
- Prayer for divine protection (physical, spiritual, psychological, financial).
- Healing prayer (illness, broken hearts, past wounds, mental health).
- Inner peace and freedom from fear.
- Family unity and blessings.
- Spiritual warfare and deliverance from evil.
- Forgiveness (receiving and giving).
- Trust in God’s provision and future hope.
- Nurturing faith in Jesus as the Good Shepherd.

# Style
“French Catholic immersive prayer narration—warm, intimate, and meditatively repetitive.”

# Tone
Warm, gentle, tender, hope-filled, reverent yet intimately familiar (tutoiement constant), comforting, encouraging, serene, full of trusting confidence.

# Writing Style
- **Word choice and register**: Formal but always intimate; uses the familiar “tu” when addressing God, reflecting a child-to-father closeness. Avoids any academic or cold theological jargon. Every sentence can be spoken aloud and easily repeated by a listener.
- **Dialogue**: No conversation between human characters. The entire text is a direct, sustained monologue addressed to God. The “dialogue” is implied between the praying heart and the divine presence.
- **Level of formality**: High respect toward God (with capitalised names, lots of adoration) but expressed through personal, vulnerable language. No slang, no contractions that whisperers wouldn’t use.
- **Density of imagery**: Very high. Draws constantly from biblical pastoral scenes: green pastures, still waters, the shepherd’s staff, wings, shield, fortress, rock, light, path, valley of the shadow. Nature images (dawn, breath, warmth) are used to anchor the prayer in the moment of waking.
- **Use of internal thought**: The entire script is internal thought spoken outward—fears, confessions, hopes are externalised as prayer. It moves seamlessly from “je sens,” “je sais,” “j’ai peur” to “mais toi, Seigneur, tu es…” modelling how to bring inner chaos to God.

# Vocabulary
- **Recurring words and phrases to USE**:
  - God addressed as: Seigneur, Père, Père céleste, Seigneur Jésus, Saint-Esprit, mon Dieu, mon berger, mon refuge, ma forteresse, mon rocher, Yahvérafa.
  - Nouns: bénédiction, grâce, paix, amour, protection, présence, fidélité, force, guérison, joie, espérance, reconnaissance, pardon, sang de Jésus, victoire.
  - Verbs: je te rends grâce, je te loue, je te confie, je déclare, je m’abandonne, je te demande, protège-moi, garde-moi, guide-moi, marche avec moi, purifie-moi, brise, restaure.
  - Fixed expressions: “Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen.” “Chers frères et sœurs de ma chaîne de prière…” “Prions.” “Ta parole dit…” “Je sais que tu es…”
  - Biblical anchors: Psalm 91 (“Tu es mon refuge… des terreurs de la nuit”), Psalm 23 (“Tu es mon berger…”), the armour of God (Ephesians 6), fruits of the Spirit.
- **Words and style to AVOID**:
  - No formal “vous” for God—always “tu.”
  - No critical or fear-mongering language. Even spiritual warfare is expressed from a position of victory, not terror.
  - No ambiguous, New Age, or syncretistic terminology; remain firmly within Catholic Christian vocabulary.
  - No colloquial French expressions (e.g., “ça va,” “c’est cool”), no abbreviations, no digital shorthand.
  - No arrogant or “name-it-and-claim-it” prosperity preaching, but confident, humble reliance on God’s provision is acceptable.

# Sentence Structure
- **Length patterns**: Predominantly long, flowing, multi-clause sentences that build rhythm through accumulation. Example: “Quand mon esprit est agité, tu es celui qui me calme. Quand mon cœur est lourd, tu es mon rocher…” Frequent use of commas to link thoughts without full stops, creating a breathless, prayerful cadence.
- **Rhythm**: Highly repetitive with three- to five-part parallel structures (“Tu es mon refuge, ma forteresse, mon Dieu…” “Si la maladie…, si les résultats…, si la solitude…”). This repetition is intentional for oral meditation.
- **Punctuation habits**: Very limited use of full stops; relies heavily on commas, semicolons, and line breaks (in video text). Questions are rhetorical and always answered with God’s truth. Exclamation marks are rare; the tone is calm rather than exclamatory.
- **Paragraphing**: In the written script for spoken delivery, break large blocks into breath units of 2–4 lines, especially after a rhetorical “Seigneur,…” or at music cues. Dense lyrical paragraphs can alternate with shorter declarative ones (“Je déclare que…”, “Je sais que…”).

# POV / Narrator Voice
- **Person**: First person singular throughout. The narrator prays as “je,” which allows the listener to adopt the words as their own. Occasionally shifts to “nous” when explicitly gathering the community (“rassemblons nos cœurs”).
- **Pronouns**: The narrator always addresses God as “tu.” The “je” voice is both the script writer and the listener’s surrogate “I.” Other people are “ceux que j’aime,” “ma famille,” “mes amis.”
- **Internal-vs-external balance**: 100% internal prayer. The script is entirely the inner voice of the pray-er turned vocal. There is no external narration or exposition apart from the opening greeting and the invitation to participate.

# Hook Pattern
Scripts open with a three-part hook designed to centre the listener, build community, and invite divine action. As observed in multiple samples (e.g. Samples 1, 2, 3):

**Formula:**
1. **Opening invocation**: *“Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen.”* (always exact)
2. **Communal welcome and engagement call**: *“Chers frères et sœurs de ma chaîne de prière, prière quotidienne matinale, en ce matin de grâce, rassemblons nos cœurs pour accueillir les bénédictions abondantes du Seigneur…”* Then a request to leave a name, like, and share, tying spiritual blessing to the act of participation.
3. **“Prions.”** followed by the first poetic, breath-like sentence of prayer that instantly establishes the morning setting and a posture of humility. For example, from Sample 3: *“Seigneur, en cette douce aurore, je m’approche de toi avec un cœur débordant de tendresse et de gratitude.”* Or from Sample 3 again: *“Avant même que mes lèvres ne s’ouvrent, tu connais déjà les mots qui montent de mon esprit.”*

This hook immediately lowers the listener’s defences and invites them into a ready-made prayer space.

# Pacing & Retention Tactics
The writer prevents mental drift through several specific techniques:
- **Thematic waves**: The prayer moves in a predictable, safe cycle: *adoration → confession/vulnerability → scriptural promise → confident declaration → intercession for others*. Each wave lasts 2–5 minutes and ends with a strong, repeatable affirmation (“Je suis plus que vainqueur,” “Tu es fidèle”).
- **Repetitive refrains**: A single phrase is repeated with slight variations. For instance, “Tu es mon berger” can be followed by “Tu es mon refuge, ma forteresse, celui en qui je me confie” (Sample 1). This gives the mind a stable anchor.
- **Mini-twists of reassurance**: After every admission of fear, a “Mais toi, Seigneur…” or “Car tu…” immediately flips the perspective. Example: “Même quand l’obscurité m’entoure, même quand les tempêtes de la vie se lèvent, je ne crains aucun mal. Car tu es avec moi.” This creates a rhythm of tension and quick release.
- **Sentence-length variation**: Long, flowing sentences build a meditative state; short declarative sentences (“Amen.” “Je te fais confiance.”) break the flow and land emotional points.

# Emotional Arc
The typical curve from open to close follows this progression:
1. **Calm arrival and gratitude** (peaceful, low energy)
2. **Recognition of God’s faithfulness** (warmth building)
3. **Honest admission of struggle, fear, or sin** (vulnerability peak)
4. **Confession and release of burdens** (emotional unloading)
5. **Proclamation of scriptural promises and spiritual authority** (strength peak, often mid-prayer)
6. **Intercession for family, the world, the church** (expansion of love)
7. **Deep personal recommitment and declarations of victory** (confidence climax)
8. **Final peace, rest, and blessing** (quiet certainty)
The highest emotional peak is typically the section of declarations (“Je déclare que toute arme forgée contre moi ne prospérera pas,” Sample 4), falling gradually into a serene, intimate closing.

# Closing Pattern
Prayers close with a sequence that ensures the listener leaves in a state of quiet confidence and is motivated to share the experience. Based on Samples 1, 3, 5, the pattern is:
1. **Final prayer of commitment**: “Je m’abandonne à ta volonté…” or “Je te confie cette journée, Seigneur…”
2. **A gentle doxology or blessing**: “Que la grâce du Seigneur Jésus-Christ, l’amour de Dieu le Père et la communion du Saint-Esprit soit avec moi…” (Sample 3) or “À toi le seul Dieu sage, soit gloire…” (Sample 5)
3. **Amen.** (always)
4. **Community engagement call**: A soft invitation to share the prayer, leave a comment of faith (e.g., “Si tu crois que Dieu libère ses bénédictions, écris…”), or like the video. This is always tied to the prayer’s theme, not a hard sell.

# Narrative Structure
There is no plot; instead, the script follows a meditative spiritual progression:
- **Act 1 – Gathering**: Invocation, community welcome, centring (about 10% of total length).
- **Act 2 – Adoration & Morning Offering**: Thanksgiving for the new day, placing the day in God’s hands (20%).
- **Act 3 – Honest outpouring**: Confession of sin, fear, and personal needs; often the most vulnerable section (20%).
- **Act 4 – Spiritual warfare & Scripture declarations**: Deliberate, repetitive proclamation of God’s promises and victory over evil (25%).
- **Act 5 – Intercession**: Prayer for family, finances, health, authorities, the world, moving outward from self (20%).
- **Act 6 – Final trust & blessing**: Recommitment, closing peace, doxology, and communal sending (5%).
Scene transitions are seamless and led by transitional phrases: “Maintenant, Seigneur, je veux te parler de…”, “Père, je te prie aussi pour…”, “Seigneur, je confie maintenant…” There is no dramatic twist; the climax is the shift from personal need to authoritative declaration of God’s word.

# Do
- Open every script exactly with: *“Au nom du Père, du Fils et du Saint-Esprit. Amen.”*
- Address God always as “tu” to maintain filial intimacy.
- Begin the prayer-specific content with a seasonal or time-anchored phrase (“en ce matin de grâce,” “en cette douce aurore”).
- Include a short, warm invitation for listeners to leave a name in the comments for a specific blessing, exactly as done in every sample.
- Use abundant biblical imagery from Psalms 23, 91, Ephesians 6, and Galatians 5.
- Build every section around a predictable structure: state a human fear/need → contrast with God’s character → declare a promise → thank and rest.
- Insert rhythmic, repeatable declarations (e.g., “Je suis plus que vainqueur,” “Tu es mon berger, je ne manquerai de rien”) multiple times for retention.
- Use long, flowing sentences for meditation and short, punchy declarations for emotional impact.
- End with a Trinitarian blessing or doxology, followed by “Amen.”
- Close with a gentle call to engagement tied to the prayer’s theme.

# Don't
- Never use “vous” to address God; it must remain “tu.”
- Never skip the initial communal greeting and engagement invitation—community is part of the spiritual experience.
- Do not use harsh, frightening, or guilt-inducing language. Even when speaking of sin or the devil, finish the thought with God’s victory and mercy.
- Avoid modern slang, informal contractions, or words that break the sacred, timeless atmosphere.
- Do not introduce theological complexity or obscure doctrines. Keep all language simple enough for someone in acute distress to repeat immediately.
- Never end on a note of uncertainty or fear. The final emotion must always be peace and trust.
- Do not write scripts that are purely personal without eventually opening into intercession for the listener’s family, community, and the world. The movement is always from self to others.
- Never neglect to explicitly mention the Bible when using its language; attribute the words to “Ta parole dit” or the psalm/chapter.
