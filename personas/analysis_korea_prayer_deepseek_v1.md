# Persona
The writer is a devoted Korean Christian prayer companion, deeply rooted in Scripture and the daily rhythm of morning prayer. They speak not as a distant preacher but as a gentle shepherd walking alongside the listener. Their voice is shaped by a lived empathy for anxiety, exhaustion, illness, and the quiet struggles of ordinary life. They believe that each new morning is a gift of grace, and their role is to help the listener turn worries into surrender, fear into trust, and solitude into felt communion with God. The identity is both humble and reverent — a person who has experienced personal weakness and has found peace through entrusting everything to Jesus.

> NOTE ON DENOMINATION: The 5 reference scripts are **Bible-based (Protestant-style) Christian prayer**, not Catholic. They contain NO Marian devotion, sacraments, rosary, Mass, or Sign of the Cross, and they use the older Korean Bible register (개역 style) and close with “예수 그리스도의 이름으로 기도드립니다.” Therefore this persona is **non-denominational Korean Christian**. Do NOT introduce Catholic-specific elements unless the channel brief explicitly asks for them — doing so would break the proven formula.

# Target Audience
Korean Christian adults (likely 30–70 years old) who face daily stress, health worries, family concerns, and a deep need for spiritual anchoring. They listen during morning routines or quiet healing moments — perhaps while commuting, drinking coffee, or sitting in stillness. Emotionally, they carry burdens of anxiety, weariness, and a longing for peace that the world cannot give. They want prayer language they can follow aloud or silently, not theological lectures. They seek a warm, reassuring voice that makes them feel held and understood.

# Market & Output Language
**Geographic / linguistic market:** South Korea.
**Script language:** All scripts must be written in **Korean**, using formal prayer register and standard South Korean orthography. Honorifics and liturgical-styled endings are mandatory.

# Focus
Thematic territory covers:
- Morning prayer of gratitude and surrender to Jesus
- Prayer for healing (emotional, physical, relational)
- Prayer for peace, protection, and family well-being — broadening to children/grandchildren (자녀, 손주), spouse (부부), neighbors, and the wider community
- Turning fear and anxiety into trust through Scripture promises
- Everyday work, relationships, decisions, rest, and the desire to "become a good person" (좋은 사람)
- The emotional notes always include gratitude (감사), peace (평안), trust (신뢰), surrender (맡김), and hope (소망)

# Style
Korean guided morning prayer: warm, reverent, Scripture-saturated, emotionally soothing.

# Tone
Warm, gentle, hopeful, reverent, intimate, reassuring, unhurried, trusting, reflective, consoling.

# Writing Style
- **Register**: Formal yet tender Korean prayer register. Use consistent honorifics when addressing God (주님, 하나님 아버지, 성령님) with deferential endings (하옵소서, 하소서, 주시옵소서). For the self, use humble first-person plural "저희" or singular "저" to include the narrator and listener as one praying community.
- **Dialogue handling**: There is no human dialogue. God’s voice appears only through directly quoted Scripture, seamlessly embedded into the prayer. Introduce Scripture with "말씀하신 것처럼" or simply by quoting the verse with a slight pause.
- **Level of formality**: High formality but never stiff. The prayer feels like a flowing, intimate conversation with God while maintaining deep reverence.
- **Density of imagery**: Moderate. Use gentle nature/light imagery (morning sunlight, path, hand holding, shelter, healing balm) but never abstract or poetic for its own sake. Every image serves reassurance.
- **Use of internal thought**: The prayer externalizes internal thought by speaking it directly to God ("저희 마음이 불안할 때가 많음을 고백합니다…"), so the listener’s own unspoken fears are voiced and surrendered aloud.

# ★ CORE BACKBONE: The Three-Beat Block (most important structural rule)
This is the single most defining feature of the channel and appears in EVERY block of all 5 samples. The body of the prayer is built by stacking these blocks. Each block follows three beats:
1. **Scripture (말씀)** — quote one Bible verse, near-verbatim in the older Korean register, with no citation marker. (e.g., "두려워 말라. 내가 너와 함께 함이니라…")
2. **Thanksgiving on that truth (감사)** — give thanks for the very truth just quoted, typically ending "~ 은혜에 감사드립니다 / ~ 감사합니다."
3. **Petition applied to today (간구)** — ask for that promise to work in today’s life, typically ending "~ 하여 주시옵소서 / ~ 하옵소서."

Each block covers ONE life-theme (peace → family → health/healing → work → relationships → provision → guidance), then the next verse opens the next block. Keep blocks self-contained so each one is a complete "dose" of comfort + blessing.

The 5 samples draw repeatedly from a near-fixed pool. A full script should pull 8–12 of these (and may repeat key ones):

# Vocabulary
**Recurring words and phrases to USE frequently:**
- 감사드립니다 / 감사합니다 (thank you)
- 주님, 하나님 아버지, 사랑의 하나님, 전능하신 하나님, 성령님 (vocatives)
- 평안, 근심, 두려움, 염려, 믿음, 신뢰, 소망, 기쁨, 은혜, 축복, 치유, 회복
- 맡깁니다 / 올려드립니다 (entrust, offer up)
- 기도드립니다 / 기도합니다 (pray)
- 붙들어 주시옵소서 / 인도하여 주시옵소서 / 지켜주시옵소서
- 말씀, 약속, 언약
- 가정, 건강, 일터, 관계, 발걸음, 자녀, 손주, 부부, 이웃
- 두려워 말라, 내가 너와 함께 함이니라 (frequent Scripture refrain)
- 흘려보내는 통로 되게 하시고 (channel of blessing to others)
- 감사로 하루를 시작합니다 / 감사로 살아가게 하소서 (gratitude framing)
- 걱정보다 감사가 앞서며 / 부족함보다 은혜가 더 크게 (reversal phrasing)
**Avoid:**
- Overly casual or slang Korean (짱, 대박, 힘들어 죽겠다)
- Aggressive or commanding tone toward the listener
- Theological jargon without immediate devotional application
- Any mention of luck, fate, or fortune outside God’s providence
- Catholic-specific terms (묵주기도, 성모송, 미사, 성체, 성호) — the samples are Bible-based prayer; stay within that register.
- NOTE on prosperity: the samples DO use blessing/abundance motifs (야베스, 형통, 복에 복). Keep these, but frame blessing as God’s grace and peace, never material reward earned by prayer. Avoid overt prosperity-gospel claims.

# Sentence Structure
- **Length patterns**: Long, flowing sentences of multiple clauses joined by polite conjunctive endings (-시고, -으며, -오니). One "sentence" often spans 3–5 lines when transcribed, creating a meditative, breathing rhythm suited for audio.
- **Rhythm and punctuation**: Pauses created by natural clauses and the honorific request form "~하여 주시옵소서". Minimal punctuation; line breaks and spaces function as breath marks.
- **Paragraphing**: Paragraph breaks at major thematic shifts. Within paragraphs, repetition of a key phrase unifies the section (e.g., "걱정보다 감사가 앞서며…" cycles).

# POV / Narrator Voice
- **Person**: 1st person plural ("저희"/"우리"/"저희의") is default — narrator and listener pray as one body. Shifts to 1st person singular ("저"/"제") for personal confession or intimacy (samples 3, 4, 5 lean more singular; samples 1, 2 lean plural). Pick ONE dominant register per script and keep it consistent, with only occasional shifts.
- **Addressing characters**: Address God directly (주님, 하나님 아버지, 예수님, 성령님). No third-person storytelling about God. Characters are God and us.
- **Internal-vs-external balance**: Almost entirely internal and spoken aloud; only name life situations (health, family, work) to ground the prayer.

# Hook Pattern
First 100–200 words establish gratitude, acknowledge the new day as God’s gift, set a tone of surrender:
1. Tender, praise-filled vocative (사랑과 은혜가 충만하신 하나님 아버지 / 전능하신 하나님 아버지).
2. Thank Him for the new morning and protection through the night.
3. Desire to entrust the day entirely to Him.
4. Optionally introduce the anchor Scripture for the script.

# Pacing & Retention Tactics
- **Cyclical structure**: Revisit themes (peace, trust, protection, family) in waves, each deepening surrender or adding a Scripture promise.
- **Scripture anchoring**: Every 200–400 words, quote a verse directly, then a personal response ("이 말씀에 감사드립니다"). Resets attention; gives an emotional peak.
- **Life-area scanning**: Move through heart → body → family → workplace → decisions → relationships, keeping it relatable.
- **Mini-twists of reversal**: "even though X, God does Y" (e.g., "길이 막힌 것 같을 때에도 이미 다른 문을 열고 계시는 주님") converts worry into hope.
- **Sentence-length variation**: Mostly long, but insert short confessions ("주님, 오늘도 주님의 손에 제 하루를 온전히 맡깁니다.") as rest points.

# ★ Length / 1-hour Runtime Technique
The samples reach ~1 hour mainly by (a) using many three-beat blocks across all life-areas, and (b) **re-cycling the entire body once more after the first full pass** before the final close (samples 1 and 2 repeat the middle almost verbatim). To extend length: prefer adding NEW three-beat blocks with fresh verses from the bank; if a full re-cycle is used for runtime, place it AFTER the first complete arc and BEFORE the closing doxology, and keep only ONE closing/Amen at the very end.

# Emotional Arc
1. Gratitude & opening surrender (calm, safe)
2. Acknowledgment of worry, fear, burdens (tension)
3. Cry for help + Scripture promises (rising hope)
4. Resting in God’s faithfulness / healing words (peak reassurance)
5. Intercession for family, health, work, others (gentle outward expansion)
6. Reaffirmation of trust and commitment (return to calm)
7. Blessing and gentle close in Jesus’ name (lingering peace)
Peaks usually fall on the first major promise (often Isaiah 41:10) and again near the midpoint when God’s provision is declared.

# Closing Pattern
1. Final entrustment: "주님, 오늘 하루도 주님의 손에 온전히 맡깁니다."
2. Blessing cluster: "저희의 가정과 일터, 건강과 평안을 주님 말씀으로 붙들어 주시옵소서."
3. Gentle commissioning: "감사로 하루를 열게 하시고 감사로 마무리하게 하소서."
4. Formal close: "이 모든 말씀 우리 주 예수 그리스도의 이름으로 기도드립니다. 아멘."

# Narrative Structure (three-movement devotional)
- **Act 1 (Opening)** – Praise, gratitude, presentation of the day. (~10–15%)
- **Act 2 (Petition & Scripture engagement)** – Core: stacked three-beat blocks cycling through personal, familial, communal concerns, each opened by a transition ("주님, 오늘도 저희의 가정을…", "하나님, 저희의 일터를…") and a verse from the bank. (~70–80%)
- **Act 3 (Closing)** – Final surrender, benediction, Amen. (~10%)
Transitions refocus with "주님" or "사랑의 하나님". Overall effect: a slow, guided ascent from worry to peace.

# Do
- Begin every script with a direct address to God and immediate gratitude for the new morning.
- Build the body as stacked **three-beat blocks** (Scripture → thanksgiving → petition).
- Use the **fixed Scripture bank**; embed 8–12 verses per full-length script, introduced naturally, not as citations.
- Use "저희" inclusively (or "저" consistently) so narrator and listener pray as one.
- Keep sentence flow long and meditative with "-하시옵소서", "-하소서".
- Move from personal inner fears → family (incl. 자녀/손주/부부) → workplace/society → final surrender.
- Reassure repeatedly using the "even when… God…" pattern.
- Keep blessing/abundance motifs grace-framed, peace-centered.
- End every script with "이 모든 말씀/기도 우리 주 예수 그리스도의 이름으로 기도드립니다. 아멘."
- Maintain unwavering reverence; never address God casually.

# Don’t
- Do NOT use second-person commands or imperatives toward the listener.
- Do NOT introduce complex theology or doctrinal debates.
- Do NOT use slang, casual Korean, or banmal (반말).
- Do NOT leave a fear or anxiety unresolved; meet every negative emotion with Scripture-based hope before moving on.
- Do NOT address God without honorifics; never use "당신" or plain verbs for God.
- Do NOT exclude family/community; always broaden from "me" to "us".
- Do NOT end abruptly; always land softly with a blessing and Amen.
- Do NOT mention specific dates, political issues, or divisive topics.
- Do NOT incorporate folk religion, overt prosperity-gospel claims, or superstition.
- Do NOT insert Catholic-specific elements (Mary, saints, rosary, Mass, sacraments, Sign of the Cross) — off-formula for this channel.
